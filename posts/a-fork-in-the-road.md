I'm burnt out, I'm disenchanted by technology, and I'm disappointed in .

Nothing works anymore, 

I'm tired of being seen as just a developer.

Since 2014, I've been paid for my skills as a front-end engineer. This has included roles of various levels with varying levels of influence, but it's almost always been about my expertise in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.









I'm unhappy and I feel stuck. There, I said it.

When that first sentence came to me when writing this post, my brain's alarm bells went off: "You can't say that! No one wants to read depressing shit on someone's blog." I recognized my brain was warning me and I pushed forward anyway. It felt good.



I glance at the LinkedIn feed of my recommended jobs to see what's new:

- Staff Frontend Engineer
- Senior Software Engineer - Frontend
- UI Engineer

I audibly sigh.

Earlier in my life, I would've been surged with energy at seeing these and applied to each of them in earnest.

But that was before.

Today, I look at this feed and see a bunch of roles with the same old job titles, the same scope of work, and teams running a similar technology stack.

Suddenly, I catch myself. My consciousness wakes up.

"Why does this bother you?" it asks.

"I'm sick of the same old role." I answer.



--

I still love to code. It's a type of creative outlet for me. From redesigning my website to use new language features in CSS to trying out new frameworks and web APIs in side projects, I find coding is still an opportunity for me to continue expanding my knowledge. I'll never tire of it.

What doesn't excite me about it is solving the same old problems, having the same old conversations, and 

In my eyes, this is the third fork in the road. The first was in college, the second was at RBC, and this is the most recent.

I started tinkering with the World Wide Web back in 2003–2004. I themed InvisionFree message boards using simple CSS to change the way they looked. This was cool to me, but at that time I was more interested in chatting on the message boards to swap trading cards than I was with theming.

Flash forward to 2010 and I was reintroduced to the world of web development through a course in my Advertising program in college. My interest in using code to design was reignited and I immediately pivoted to a program called Computer Systems Analysis at a different college.
