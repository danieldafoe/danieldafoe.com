In 2017, while working at Canada's largest bank, agile product development was being introduced by [McKinsey][mckinsey] to our organization.

One of the more misunderstood roles that came along with the agile product development package was that of the Scrum Master.

A wonderful idea McKinsey had in order to help people better understand the role of a Scrum Master was for everyone to get an opportunity to assume the role for a sprint—a two week time span.

As someone who enjoys organization, optimization, and communication, I was naturally very interested in this role and gladly took on more cycles when others didn't want to.

Now later into my career, I find myself thinking back to the useful, transferrable traits of that role and how helpful they've been throughout my career.

## 1. Creative problem solving
When working as a Scrum Master, one of your goals is to remove blockers and impediments to the product team accomplishing their tasks.

A typical issue is having external technical dependencies on other teams. This could be due to numberous different reasons. Some reasons this can cause trouble for your team are:
- Prioritization of activities not in alignment with your team, meaning 
- If another team's work isn't done in time for the tasks in your sprint, you jeopardize your delivery of those features

## 2. Knowledge of the whole
When working as an individual contributor at a company, it's easy to be concerned only with the outputs of your own role. 

Are you a UX researcher? Find out what problems people have. Are you a visual designer? Bring life to the UI. Are you a front-end engineer? Build the web app. Are you a tester? Make sure the app isn't broken.

In the role of a Scrum Master, however, you need to have a holistic understanding of how everyone's outputs come together to form a singular outcome—and it's your job to ensure that outcome is met.

You need to have an understanding of how each piece fits together, the inputs that allow one person in their role to begin their work, the interdependencies at play between roles, and the potential risks for each role.

This deep understanding of the entire team and its process gives you a unique insight that some in the sport world might call "seeing the whole field". This macro level vision positioned the Scrum Master to notice threats and issues before anyone else, including areas for improvement.


## Be your own scrum master
Regardless of your current role, you can take these useful traits of a Scrum Master into your role. It will make you a more capable and valuable team member.

Try it out by starting small: When you need something from another team with a different velocity than your own, find ways to lessen or remove your dependency on the team. 

Is your backend API not ready for front-end devs to test it? Mock the interaction and response with your own mock server.

Is your design system slow to create a variation of a component you need? Make the change for your use case and contribute it back to the design system. If the component gets feedback from the design team, incorporate those changes back into your experience in the next iteration of your feature.
