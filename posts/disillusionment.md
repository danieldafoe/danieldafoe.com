  The tech bubble of my mind has burst. It's not the magical, unicorn-laden world I once constructed. It's banal, uninspired drivel masquerading as revolutionary.
</div>

There was a time, in about 2011, that I was inspired and enamoured by the world of technology and technology companies.

I remember fervently smashing the follow button on all my favourite technology companies on Twitter when I joined in 2010, and again smashed more follow buttons when I found people who worked at these fabled companies. (Big companies like IBM even followed me back!)

"Amazing! Wonderful!" I cried.

In the midst of completing a college program about computer systems analysis, I gleefully imagined how exciting it would be to work on these incredible digital and physical products these companies produced.

"What a dream!" I exclaimed.


## The great unfollowing

After a hiatus, I recently returned to Twitter (X). This choice, along with many others this year, is thanks to my renewed focus on self-reflection and self-growth. You might think that's an odd reason to return to a tarnished social media network, and you're right, it is. I'll post another day about how I arrived at that decision.

Back to the story.

I stepped into the wilted cornfield of my Twitter feed, hoping to find some semblance of people I follow debating the latest CSS spec or talking about a revolutionary browser coming soon.

I did find those things. And they didn't excite me.

I flicked my screen again and again, diving deep into my curated 2:1 newsfeed of tweets vs. ads, desperate to find something to latch onto or be excited about. Instead, I found myself bored—like I'd stepped into a room of people talking about how cool a video game from 10 years ago is. A game I'd already played for countless hours.

What happened next was something I never thought I'd do: I did a purge of accounts I was following.

I had considered this once before, but last time I tried, I felt a sting of emotional attachment because my following list is in chronological order and it was neat to be able to see the first people and companies I followed when I first got Twitter. It's like a time capsule of seasons of my life. I followed these companies and people when I was in advertising, these ones when I pivoted to software engineering, and these when I got into accessibility. It's a fascinating glimpse into the different eras of my life—like a mini Taylor Swift discography.

This time, however, I went through with the unfollowing. 

I was ruthless. I cut out tech companies, companies that talk about tech companies, tech workers, and some old college friends who had stopped posting back in 2012.


## "Twitter just sucks now"
Sure, some of this disinterest in Twitter content could be due to the people I was following or the mass exodus of people with interesting content who flocked to other platforms, but I still saw some familiar faces posting from before. They were still posting about web development or Linux distros like before, too. 

It was me who had changed.

## Same old song and dance
I've now experienced for myself how these tech companies I once idolized work. To my dismay, it was uninspiring. Working on a digital product team for a bank in Canada felt more alive and exciting than a Silicon Valley tech company. Go figure.

I know I can't speak for all tech companies in the Greater San Francisco Bay Area. There's bound to be some amazing companies that work differently (effectively) and foster inspiring work cultures. In fact, I know there are. Brands like [37signals][37signals] do just that.

## Where the wild ideas are
I remember when the rabbit r1 and the Humane AI pin were launched. My partner and I pre-ordered one. Were we enamoured by the magical keynotes? Probably. But I was also excited to see something new: a compact piece of circuits wrapped in a neat design with a promise to make the UX of everyday life even better.

These sorts of radical departures from the screen-and-keys of a laptop, to me, are such a fresh perspective. To have something that rips me away from this tired form factor is always welcome. Handheld gaming systems, novelty...

Maybe I'm just tired of software. 

## Great(ish) expectations
To be clear: I still love and believe in the power of digital technology and its ability to do good. It has changed the world in many ways already—I think for the better.
